# Cross-camera Performance of Deep Learning Algorithms to Diagnose Common Ophthalmic Diseases: A Comparative Study Highlighting Feasibility to Portable Fundus Camera Use

**Citation:** He S, Bulloch G, Zhang L, Xie Y, Wu W, He Y, Meng W, Shi D, He M. Cross-camera Performance of Deep Learning Algorithms to Diagnose Common Ophthalmic Diseases: A Comparative Study Highlighting Feasibility to Portable Fundus Camera Use. Current Eye Research. 2023;48(9):857-863. doi: 10.1080/02713683.2023.2215984. Venue: Current Eye Research. **Peer reviewed.**
**Year:** 2023
**Dataset(s) used:** Fundus images of the same eye conditions captured with different cameras, including a portable fundus camera
**Model(s)/method(s):** Deep learning algorithms for common eye diseases, compared across camera types

## Summary (plain English)
This study asked a simple practical question: if you take an AI system built for one camera and feed it pictures from another camera, does it still work? A fundus camera photographs the back of the eye. The team compared how the same algorithms performed across camera types, including a portable one that can be carried to patients rather than the patient coming to it. The goal was to judge whether portable cameras are good enough to be used with existing AI.

## Key finding (plain English)
The authors conclude that using a portable fundus camera with these deep learning algorithms is feasible, while documenting that performance is not identical across cameras.

## Relevance to my research (plain English)
**Partially conflicts with my expected result, which makes it valuable.** Most papers I found emphasise a drop when moving to portable cameras. This one leans towards "portable is workable."
