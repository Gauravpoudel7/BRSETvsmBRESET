# Artificial Intelligence Tools for Diabetic Retinopathy Screening in Low-Resource Environments: Review of Global Implementation and Challenges

**Citation:** Grace M, Estil-las A, Arsene C. Artificial Intelligence Tools for Diabetic Retinopathy Screening in Low-Resource Environments: Review of Global Implementation and Challenges. The Columbia University Journal of Global Health. 2026;15(2). doi: 10.52214/cujgh.v15i2.14168. Venue: Columbia University Journal of Global Health. **Peer reviewed.**
**Year:** 2026
**Dataset(s) used:** No new data. Reviews five deployed systems: IDx-DR, EyeArt, Medios AI, Google's Automated Retinal Disease Assessment, and SELENA+
**Model(s)/method(s):** Narrative review of performance, global implementation and barriers

## Summary (plain English)
This review looks at the five best-known AI screening products and asks what happens when they meet real health systems in poorer settings. It separates two different questions: can the technology work, and can it actually be delivered. On delivery it covers barriers that have nothing to do with accuracy, such as unreliable electricity and internet, regulatory approval, and whether clinicians and patients will trust and use the tool. It also surveys proposed fixes, including federated learning, which lets models learn across hospitals without moving patient data, offline-capable devices, and AI whose reasoning is easier to understand.

## Key finding (plain English)
These systems can raise detection rates, cut referral delays and improve access, but their integration is blocked by four recurring barriers: data generalisability, infrastructure, regulation, and clinician and patient acceptance.
